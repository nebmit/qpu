02Context

We have quantum computers.
They're noisy.

100 – 1000+ qubits commercially available today
Gate error rates: 0.1 – 1%
Coherence times: 10 – 1000 μs
This is the Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum (NISQ) era. Circuits must complete before decoherence destroys the computation — and not every qubit on a chip performs equally well.
03Background

The quantum bit

A classical bit is exactly 0 or 1
A qubit exists in a superposition of both simultaneously
Measurement collapses the state to a definite 0 or 1
N qubits span a superposition of 2N basis states
|ψ⟩ = α|0⟩ + β|1⟩
where |α|² + |β|² = 1
Bloch sphere — qubit state space
04Background

Quantum states are fragile

Interaction with the environment destroys quantum information. Two key timescales:
T₁ — Relaxation
Excited state |1⟩ decays to ground state |0⟩
ibm_fez median T₁ = 123 μs
T₂ — Dephasing
Phase coherence is lost — superposition decays
ibm_fez median T₂ = 101 μs
↳ Your circuit must finish in ≪ T₂ — otherwise quantum information is lost to noise before the computation completes.

06Background

Two sources of error

Recorded at every calibration — per qubit and per coupling — each a probability of something going wrong
Readout Error · per qubit
When you measure a qubit, the detector can give the wrong answer. You prepare |0⟩ but read "1", or vice versa. This happens at the very last step — the quantum computation may have been perfect.
ibm_fez range: 0.39% – 50.4%*
Gate Error · per coupling
Every gate is an imperfect physical rotation. Single-qubit gates are highly accurate; two-qubit entangling gates are harder to implement and their errors dominate circuit noise as circuits grow.
ibm_fez 2Q gate range: 0.14% – 100%*
* extreme values (≈50% readout, 100% gate error) mark dead qubits and couplings — excluded by filtering

10How it works · 1 of 3

① Filter hard

readout ≤ 12%
2Q error ≤ 4%
T₁ ≥ 100 μs
T₂ ≥ 50 μs
Qubits and couplings that miss any threshold are removed entirely — not downweighted. For the search they simply don't exist.
A flood-fill then labels the surviving connected components
Largest component < N? Fail fast — and suggest which single threshold to relax
Default thresholds on the real ibm_fez corner (2026-04-27): 18 of 42 qubits removed
11How it works · 2 of 3

② Score what survives

Every surviving qubit and coupling gets a quality score in [0, 1] — a weighted blend of its normalized calibration metrics.
Qubit quality
40%readout error
30%T₁ relaxation
30%T₂ dephasing
Candidate gain — used while growing a cluster
60%qubit quality
40%connecting 2Q-edge quality
Normalized over the surviving pool — not the whole device
0 · worst in pool missing value → 0.5 (neutral) 1 · best in pool
Error metrics are inverted — low error scores high. Unmeasured values score a neutral 0.5: unknown is not the same as bad. Re-normalizing per snapshot means scores adapt as the hardware drifts.
12How it works · 3 of 3

③ Grow greedily — 14 seeds, best wins

Seed
Start from a top-quality qubit whose degree fits the topology
Grow
Add the frontier neighbour with the highest gain, one at a time
Complete
Stop at N members — short clusters are discarded, never returned
Repeat × 14 seeds
Keep the complete cluster with the best overall score
Compact
Gain counts all connecting edges — rewards dense blocks
Linear
−0.5 gain for branching a chain · +0.05 for extending an endpoint
Branched
+0.05 for forming a junction — quality still dominates
